Cards
One roll of 1-10 is one card. 1 is an Ace, 10 is a face card. Closest to 21 without going over wins. Why: a 1-10 roll gives every value the same 10% chance and the roll is shown in chat (to everyone in say range for Say and Tell, to the group for Party and Alliance), so nobody has to trust a hidden deck.
Ace counts 1 or 11
It counts as 11 whenever that does not bust you. Why: the Ace is never a bad card, so a hand with one is 'soft' and can take a card without busting.
Tens are rarer here
A deck has four 10-value ranks (10, J, Q, K), so 31% of its cards are tens; a roll of 10 is 10%. The average card is 5.5 here and 6.5 in a deck. Why: 20s and naturals come up less often, and the dealer busts less: 22.4% of dealer hands bust here, 28.2% with a deck.
The dealer draws to 17
Below 17 the dealer must hit; on 17 or more the dealer must stand. Why: a fixed rule means the dealer makes no choices and cannot favour anyone. 17 is the standard casino line.
Dealer hits soft 17 (setting)
Makes the dealer draw again on Ace+6. Why: it raises the house edge from 1.67% to 3.27%, so it is off by default. It costs far more here than with a deck (0.49% to 0.71%): from soft 17 the next card makes 18-21 40% of the time here, 31% with a deck.
You act first
If you bust you lose, even if the dealer busts afterwards. Why: this is where the house's edge comes from in blackjack.
Double
First two cards only: the stake doubles, you take exactly one card, then stand. Why: it is meant for a strong start such as 10 or 11. With 1-10 rolls it never has the best expected value, under any setting: an 11 becomes 21 only 10% of the time (31% with a deck), so one card at double stake is worse than hitting freely.
Split
Only on a pair, once per round. Each card starts its own hand. Why: a pair like 8+8 makes a poor 16, but two hands starting from 8 can still win. One split keeps the round short. The chart below shows which pairs to split.
Double after split (setting)
Lets each split hand double on its first two cards. With 1-10 rolls it changes nothing, because doubling never pays here. With a deck it is worth 0.12% to the player.
Blackjack (natural)
Ace + 10 on your first two cards: 2.0% of hands here, 4.7% with a deck. It pays 3:2 by default and ties a dealer natural. Why: it is the best hand and rare, so it pays more. A 21 after a split is an ordinary 21: it is not your original two-card hand (the standard casino rule).
21 stands automatically
Why: no card can improve 21.
The maths (current settings, best play)
House edge
You lose 1.67% of every unit staked on average: 1.7 units per 100. The same rules with a deck: 0.49%. By natural payout: 3:2 1.67%, 6:5 2.26%, 2:1 0.69%, 1:1 2.65%.
Luck against the edge
One round swings about 0.98 units either way (standard deviation). Over n rounds the edge costs n x 1.67% of a unit, while luck spreads about 0.98 x sqrt(n), so the edge only outweighs luck after about 3,450 rounds.
Busting when you hit
A hard total t of 12 or more busts on the next card (t - 11) x 10% of the time: 12 10%, 14 30%, 16 50%, 20 90%. With a deck: 12 31%, 16 62%. That is why the chart hits more than a casino chart does.

Channels
Say rolls /random (say radius). Party and Alliance roll /dice there, so only that group sees it. Tell rolls /random and tells each card to the named player. Why: the game cannot roll into a tell, so the roll stays public and the tell only relays it.
Round result
Tick any mix of Echo (only you), Say, Party, Alliance and Tell; each gets the same one-line result.
Safety
Nothing rolls until you press a button. Every roll or post is at least the set spacing apart (2 s minimum), to stay clear of chat spam limits. Only your own roll counts as a card, so nobody else can feed you cards. If no result arrives in time, rolling pauses rather than re-rolling, so a lost roll is never counted twice. Logging out abandons the round.
Limits
English client only. Bets and gil are not handled; stakes are units. The maths assumes best play and the 1-10 rolls are fair.
