Blackjack✕
TableSettingsHelp
Cards
One roll of 1-10 is one card. 1 is an Ace, 10 is a face card. Closest to 21 without going over wins. Why: /random 10 gives every value the same 1-in-10 chance and anyone nearby can see the roll, so nobody has to trust a hidden deck.
Ace counts 1 or 11
It counts as 11 whenever that does not bust you. Why: the Ace is never a bad card, so a hand with one is 'soft' and can take a card without busting.
Tens are rarer here
A deck has four 10-value ranks (10, J, Q, K), so 31% of its cards are tens; a roll of 10 is 10%. Why it matters: 20s and naturals come up less often than at a casino table, and the dealer busts less.
The dealer draws to 17
Below 17 the dealer must hit; on 17 or more the dealer must stand. Why: a fixed rule means the dealer makes no choices and cannot favour anyone. 17 is the standard casino line.
Dealer hits soft 17 (setting)
Makes the dealer draw again on Ace+6. Why: it slightly improves the house's odds, so it is off by default.
You act first
If you bust you lose, even if the dealer busts afterwards. Why: this is where the house's edge comes from in blackjack.
Double
First two cards only: the stake doubles, you take exactly one card, then stand. Why: you are betting more on a strong start (typically 10 or 11), so you give up the right to keep hitting.
Split
Only on a pair, once per round. Each card starts its own hand. Why: a pair like 8+8 makes a poor 16, but two hands starting from 8 each can still win. One split keeps the round short.
Double after split (setting)
Lets each split hand double on its first two cards. On by default; turning it off favours the house.
Blackjack (natural)
Ace + 10 on your first two cards. It pays 3:2 by default and ties a dealer natural. Why: it is the rarest strong hand (2% here), so it pays more. 6:5 or 1:1 favours the house. A 21 after a split is an ordinary 21: it is not your original two-card hand, which is the standard casino rule.
21 stands automatically
Why: no card can improve 21.
Channels
Say rolls /random (say radius). Party and Alliance roll /dice there, so only that group sees it. Tell rolls /random and tells each card to the named player. Why: the game cannot roll into a tell, so the roll stays public and the tell only relays it.
Round result
Tick any mix of Echo (only you), Say, Party, Alliance and Tell; each gets the same one-line result.
Safety
Nothing rolls until you press a button. Every roll or post is at least the set spacing apart (2 s minimum), to stay clear of chat spam limits. Only your own roll counts as a card, so nobody else can feed you cards. If no result arrives in time, rolling pauses rather than re-rolling, so a lost roll is never counted twice. Logging out abandons the round.
Limits
English client only. Bets and gil are not handled; stakes are units.
